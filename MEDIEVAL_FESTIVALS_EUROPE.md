## ⚠️ Read this first — how to use the dates

Most of the dates below are **confirmed 2026 dates**. Since it's already late August 2026, most of them have passed. That is deliberate and still useful, because:

> **Nearly every one of these festivals runs on a fixed calendar slot** — "second weekend of July", "Pentecost weekend", "third weekend of August". The 2026 date is therefore the single most reliable predictor of the 2027 date.

Legend used throughout:

| Mark | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| ✅ **2027 confirmed** | Organiser has published the 2027 date |
| 🔁 | Fixed annual slot — 2027 will land on the same weekend ±a few days |
| 🗓️ | Date varies year to year — you must check the site |
| 🎟️ | Ticketed (rest are free or cheap walk-in) |
| ⭐ | Personal pick — worth planning a trip around |

**Rule of thumb:** 2027 programmes for the summer season get published between **January and April 2027**. Set a reminder for February.

# 🇭🇷 PART 1 — CROATIA (home turf)

Croatia's scene is smaller than Germany's but genuinely excellent — and it has two things almost nobody else has: **UNESCO-listed living knightly tournaments** (Sinjska alka, Trka na prstenac) that have run continuously for 300+ years.

## 4.4 Practical notes

- **Book accommodation early** for Visby, Landshut, Český Krumlov, Sinj and Rab — these towns fill completely.
- **Most German/Austrian markets are cash-friendly but card-patchy.** Bring cash.
- **Gewandung (costume):** many German markets offer cheaper entry in period dress; some (MPS, Castlefest) it's basically expected.
- **Free vs ticketed:** Croatian, Czech, Romanian and most Italian events are free or ~€5–10. German/Austrian ticketed shows (Kaltenberg, Rosenburg, Laxenburg) run €20–45.
- **Dog/kid friendliness** varies a lot — check individual sites.
- **The best single planning tool** is [mittelalterkalender.info](https://www.mittelalterkalender.info/) — over 1,000 events a year across Germany *and* the rest of Europe, filterable.
